The spine

Six ideas carry the whole subject. Read this list first; every page below unpacks one or two of them.

  • 1 · AmplitudesA classical probabilistic computer tracks 2ⁿ non-negative probabilities; a quantum computer tracks 2ⁿ complex amplitudes. Gates are unitary matrices instead of stochastic ones, and probabilities are squared magnitudes |a_x|². That one change is the entire difference. P1 · P4
  • 2 · InterferenceAmplitudes can be negative, so paths can cancel. A quantum algorithm is a choreography that makes wrong answers cancel and right ones add up before you measure. Superposition alone buys nothing, because measurement hands you one sample. P3 · P4
  • 3 · StructureBig speedups need problem structure. Period finding (Shor) and simulating quantum physics get exponential gains; unstructured search (Grover) gets only √N; NP-complete problems are not believed to fall at all. P2 · P5
  • 4 · ErrorsPhysical qubits fail about once per thousand operations; useful algorithms need about one failure per trillion. Error correction closes that gap by spending hundreds to thousands of physical qubits per logical qubit, and only works below a threshold error rate. P6
  • 5 · Where it stands2024–2026 proved error correction scales in principle, on tens of logical qubits. The headline numbers mean different things (detection vs correction, memory vs gates, distance 2 vs 7), and several are company claims not yet peer reviewed. P7 · P8
  • 6 · What to doFor most engineers, the actionable part is post-quantum cryptography, now. Chemistry and materials are the real long-term prize; claims about optimisation and machine learning deserve scepticism. P9
